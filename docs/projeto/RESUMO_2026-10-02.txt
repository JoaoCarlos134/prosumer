=====================================================================
1. TESTS (33 models, 3 cases, forecasting tomorrow's 24 hours at 00:00)
=====================================================================

20 of the 127 surveyed models were tested, plus references, LightGBM, a physics
model and an ensemble. That covers every family that can run on this data and
machine; the reasons for each gap are in the write-up.

Case                               Best                                Notes
---------------------------------  ----------------------------------  ------------------------------------------
Plants with tomorrow's real        Median of all models +51%,          8 models tie; LightGBM and TimesFM +48%
weather                            TFT +50%
Plants, history only               Median of all models +11%,          LSTM and GRU do worse than the simple
                                   ResNet-1D +10%                      reference (-14% and -13%)
Household solar (10 houses)        KAN +17%                            LightGBM only +6%: it learned summer and
                                                                       overpredicts winter by 40%
Household consumption (20 houses)  LightGBM +20%                       TTM and SparseTSF +19%, a statistical tie

Percentages are the gain in error (RMSE) over a simple reference. For the plants
that is yesterday's sky conditions applied to today's sun path; for the houses,
"same as yesterday". 95% intervals by day-level bootstrap are in the write-up.

Main findings
- Weather matters far more than the model. The gap between having tomorrow's
  weather and not is about 0.40; the spread between the best algorithms is about
  0.03. A linear model with a single physical variable already reaches +42%.
- Foundation models with no training tie with trained ones. TTM (3M parameters,
  runs on a CPU) is the most consistent across all three cases.
- SparseTSF, with 32 parameters, ties the best on consumption. It and TTM are the
  embedded candidates.
- The median of all models is the safest default. It was never below 2nd place,
  and it does not require picking a model.

Model improvements, in order
1. Use a real weather forecast instead of NASA (pvlib already downloads the CAMS
   satellite data).
2. A physics-plus-residual hybrid as the default.
3. The median ensemble instead of choosing one model.
4. Correct the foundation models' -20% bias on consumption.
Details are in the write-up.

Problems found along the way
- In the first run, four networks given weather inputs predicted up to 2,400 kWh
  in an hour on a 4 kW plant. It was a normalization defect in how weather inputs
  were scaled. It was fixed, checked, and all seven affected networks retrained.
- The memory guard stopped training twice. It was finished in a separate process.
- The networks did not all train under identical settings. The 9 plant networks
  with history only used the default batch sizes; the rest used smaller ones to
  save memory.
- Toto and PatchTST ran on the plants only. xLSTM did not run (it needs GPU
  kernels); the TiRex foundation model covers that family.
- Half the houses (10 of 20) have no solar panels. They are consumption-only. The
  houses also appear to be in Europe, not Brazil.


=====================================================================
2. NETWORK ANALYSIS OF THE PROJECT
=====================================================================

The graph has 1,342 nodes and 2,570 edges across code (via Graphify), data flow,
documents, literature, and findings/papers/open items. It can be browsed in
analise_rede/grafo_projeto/graphify-out/graph.html.

- The most connected file in the project does not exist on disk
  (frame5.parquet). It is an intermediate that 19 scripts depend on and nothing
  stores. Changing the start of the Cascavel chain affects about 110 files, which
  are redone by hand.
- Results reach the reports by hand. 27 analysis outputs are never read by any
  code; their numbers are typed into the reports. None of the 32 TFLite models
  has ever been loaded and run.
- The OCR paper and the Edge AI review share 21 papers, so they are one research
  line. Articles A and B have no bibliography yet.
- Curitiba's unknown geometry is the most strategic open item. It blocks both
  solar papers, and two surveyed models built for plants without metadata could
  turn it into a contribution instead.
- The OCR paper's code is not in the project. The documents cite 7 scripts and a
  simulator that do not exist here.


=====================================================================
3. LIBRARY (prossumidor)
=====================================================================

It is worth building, but as a thin layer over pvlib, NeuralForecast and the
foundation models, not a library that "solves everything" from scratch. The
evidence: the same logic is repeated across 7 to 19 scripts, and three package
conflicts broke the environment in a single day. The design covers 8 modules,
which code they would come from, a usage example, the choices the tests settled,
and about 11 weeks of work. The name is free on PyPI.
